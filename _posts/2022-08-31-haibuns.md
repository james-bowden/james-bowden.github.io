["Walking Away", C. Day Lewis](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zx8fbk7/revision/8)
> I'm kinda meh on this poem, but I like the last few lines: *How selfhood begins with a walking away, / And love is proved in the letting go.* Actually, I don't even really like that last line. Just the second to last line then.

There was a cute painting with some newts? with hoods passing balls of light between them, *Saddled with the infinite, I offer you this moment to slip between our fingers*. This is from @hammuraber on instagram.

I was also entertained because I came across a comic to the effect of *every time something bad happens to me, there is joy in that the comics are gonna be so good*, and funny, because I frame my own mishaps like this to some extent these days.

["Summer Haibun", Aimee Nezhukumatathil](https://poets.org/poem/summer-haibun)
> What a good haiku at the end, the stickiness and such. I went and actually looked up what a haibun is supposed to be in form today. Anyhow, this reminded me of the next one, my favorite (and one of only maybe 5 haibuns I've ever read, but still).

["Immigrant Haibun", Ocean Vuong](https://genius.com/Ocean-vuong-immigrant-haibun-annotated)
> How good, and in part relatable. The last verse before the haiku is one of my favorites. *If you must know anything, know that you were born because no one else was coming.* And *Maybe the body is the only question an answer can't extinguish* (I think not). And *How something that lives only to fall can be nothing but sweet*. 

["Mesilla", Carrie Fountain](https://writersalmanac.publicradio.org/index.php%3Fdate=2014%252F05%252F12.html)

I read also a little letter from Rilke to a young girl, more or less saying that what is important is that we learn to love the difficult and deal with it and such. And I am trying, I think I am well versed in this in the personal realms of life and literature/poetry, but am trying to do this more for my academic/machine learning things.
