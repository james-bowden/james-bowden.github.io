*I've recently gotten quite into classical music, and have been listening to it more frequently than singing as of late. Still, I've very little idea what I'm talking about and am looking for more compelling pieces, so please do recommend. I've gotten it into my head to pick up the cello again sometime soon, and plan to go to all the symphonies I can manage. Exciting times!

Anyhow, here are some pieces that have stuck with me, and how they came to be so--I know it's a sorry list, but I'm keeping it to only the most compelling pieces I've come across.*

* [***Introduction + Rondo Capriccioso (Op. 28)*, Saint-Saëns**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q0ajM7aL-Vc&ab_channel=HenriVieuxtemps). This one is a must-listen; please, just try it. And how can it not be great with a name like *Capriccioso*, and certainly it is that! I came across this thanks to Ellie and Sophia (a.k.a. Camille) and was an instant favorite thanks to all of the action and how distinct the sounds are.
* [***Prelude in G Minor (Op. 23 No. 5)*, Rachmaninoff**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SlcQWUn5DeI&ab_channel=Rousseau). I just love the turn in this piece--Travis introduced me to. 
* [***Liebesleid*, Kreisler (arr. Rachmaninoff)**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFvx32SjAtE&ab_channel=Rousseau). A shorter piece that Matthew also put me onto when I was getting started out. It translates to "Love's sorrow" and, well, just have a listen.
* [***Cello Concerto in B minor (Op.104)*, Dvořák**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llB7NaWLUc4&t=1s&ab_channel=HarpsichordA6). This is my other favorite cello concerto. There's so much going on, so many riveting bits interspersed with plenty of excitement but even moreso that something dark lurking, the sense of loss. This and Elgar were the "musical wallpaper", if you will, to *House of Leaves* and they're very fitting at that.
* [***Cello Concerto in E minor (Op.85)*, Elgar**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7rVW4Z70TfE&ab_channel=cello644). A lovely cello concerto that Matthew put me onto. You can tell from the first 10 seconds that it's going to be wonderful, and the refrain (is that what you call it? or a motif?) is one of the most beautiful bits I've heard. 
* [***Archduke Trio (Op. 97)*, Beethoven**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5mrfy_D9JVE). This one started it all, honestly--I listened to it after its mention in Murakami's *Kafka by the Shore* and was captivated. Many thanks to Rachel for putting me onto Murakami and thus his frequent classical music references.
