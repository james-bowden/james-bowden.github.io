## *What Is It You Feel I Asked Kurt*
#### Diane Seuss

What is it you feel I asked Kurt when you listen to<br/>
Ravel’s String Quartet in F-major, his face was so lit up<br/>
and I wondered, “the music is unlike the world I live<br/>
or think in, it’s from somewhere else, unfamiliar and unknown,<br/>
not because it is relevant to the familiar and comfortable,<br/>
but because it brings me to that place that I didn’t/couldn’t<br/>
imagine existed. And sometimes that unfamiliar place is closer<br/>
to my world than I realize, and sometimes it’s endlessly distant,”<br/>
that’s what he wrote in an email when I asked him<br/>
to remind me what he’d said earlier, off the cuff, “I don’t<br/>
recall exactly what I said,” he began, a sentence written<br/>
in iambic pentameter, and then the rest, later he spoke of two<br/>
of his brothers who died as children, leukemia and fire,<br/>
his face, soft, I’m listening to Ravel now, its irrelevancy.<br/>
