## *Diary Entry #28: Ars Poetica*
#### Diannely Antigua

I start where I am most afraid: an addiction to beauty<br/>
is a place to keep a loss. My father liked to sing love songs, his lips<br/>
elongating each vowel, his tongue breaking the center<br/>
of everything, and he—so tender to the sound it birthed.<br/>
I’m listening to jazz in the park again, crying<br/>
in rooms that don’t belong to me. It’s true—<br/>
the wrong music can be damaging and every photograph<br/>
is an elegy. I practice posing in bathroom stalls to feel<br/>
effortless. All flowers want to be looked at, and chasing the moon<br/>
is a chronic condition. How can I translate where my finger<br/>
lands into language? In conversation, I drop a little French<br/>
like a baby out the window. On TV, anyone can be dead<br/>
and look like art. A long life is avant-garde—I place mine<br/>
on the open shelf, on the edge.<br/>
