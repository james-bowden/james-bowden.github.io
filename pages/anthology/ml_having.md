## *Having "Having a Coke with You" with You*
#### Mark Leidner

You asked me if I knew the poem “Having a Coke with You”<br/>
I said I vaguely remembered it but didn’t really<br/>
so you recited it in its entirety. We were walking<br/>
from somewhere up by City Hall down toward South Street<br/>
and the whole time you were reciting it I was wondering<br/>
“Was that the last line of the poem?” after each line<br/>
and each time I thought that, I thought it even more<br/>
because as the poem got longer the fact that you were reciting it<br/>
from memory became incrementally harder to believe<br/>
until about two-thirds of the way through the poem<br/>
I stopped thinking about how long it was and just started listening<br/>
which I had been, but only a little, because of all that. Anyway<br/>
then I started listening to it completely, believing<br/>
the poem itself to be the sole reason you were reciting it<br/>
but as soon as you finished you started to talk about how<br/>
you used to think that that poem was just about how<br/>
liberatingly banal being in love with someone was<br/>
but then you said you’d started to think more recently<br/>
it was more about the idiocy of caring about art at all<br/>
when you could spend all that energy caring about someone<br/>
you loved instead, and you said you were wondering where<br/>
I stood on that question now that I had heard the poem<br/>
and I was as struck by the question as I was stunned<br/>
that you could so casually recite such a long good poem<br/>
and that you hadn't even recited it primarily to solicit<br/>
appreciation for your recitation so much as to ask<br/>
what I thought about what you had thought about it<br/>
then, versus how you thought about it now, and this was<br/>
when I knew I wanted to be with you forever.<br/>
