## On Seeing and Being Seen
#### Ama Codjoe 

I don't like being photographed. When we kissed<br/>
at a wedding, the night grew long and luminous.<br/>
You unhooked my bra. *A photograph<br/>
passes for proof*, Sontag says, *that a gien thing<br/>
has happened*. Or you leaned back to watch<br/>
as I eased the straps from my shoulders.<br/>
Hooks and eyes. Right now, my breasts<br/>
are too tender to be touched. *Their breasts<br/>
were horrifying*, Elizabeth Bishop writes. Tell her<br/>
someone wanted to touch them. I am touching<br/>
the photograph of my last seduction.  It is as slick<br/>
as a magazine page, as dark as a street<br/>
darkened by rain. When I want to remember<br/>
something beautiful, instead of taking<br/>
a photograph, I close my eyes.<br/>
I watched as you covered my nipple<br/>
with your mouth. Desire made you<br/>
beautiful. I closed my eyes.<br/>
Tonight, I am alone in my tenderness.<br/>
There is nothing in my hand except a certain<br/>
grasping. In my mind's eye, I am<br/>
stroking your hair with damp fingertips. This is exactly<br/>
how it happened. On the lit-up hotel bed,<br/>
I remember thinking, My body is a lens<br/>
I can look through with my mind.<br/>
