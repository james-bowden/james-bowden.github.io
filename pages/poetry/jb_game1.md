## The Game (I)
#### James Bowden 

The game -- what a sadomasochistic concept.<br/>
I wonder who could have first thought it up, but<br/>
I remember my first girlfriend and the world makes sense again.<br/>
You say I'm unnaturally calm, but I swear<br/>
I fought with this girl every night for a whole<br/>
semester -- mostly because she felt I didn't give<br/>
her enough time and attention, but we spent all<br/>
that I had to give being upset there wasn't<br/>
more. As high schoolers, she told me she'd liked me<br/>
since the second grade. That she and Emily,<br/>
the girl who tried to win me over with<br/>
gifts of Pokemon cards until I told her I wasn't<br/>
that into Pokemon (should have told her I<br/>
hadn't a hormone in my body at the time either),<br/>
were nemeses because of me. That once, Emily cut<br/>
her in line at the water fountain so she could<br/>
drink directly after me, and osmose whatever of my<br/>
essence (probably just sweaty saliva) had been left<br/>
behind.<br/>
That's right -- better get in line to buy my<br/>
last night's bathwater. Oh, I meant to point out<br/>
how she and I never could compromise, and thus<br/>
lost whatever game we were playing. You too --<br/>
how's that for audience relatability?<br/>

<hr>

This poem was written as a part of a coterie during my poetry class (Spring 2022) with Eve and Antonia, the topic being "the game". For the uninitiated, the game is essentially that any time you think of it, you lose. Anyhow, this is to say that much context is lost.
