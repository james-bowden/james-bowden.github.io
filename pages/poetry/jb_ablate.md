## "ablate", New Year's Day
#### James Bowden

*after Maggie Smith; for Maggie Sui*<br/>

To ablate is to remove, whether by<br/>
erosion or evaporation, by choice or by chance.<br/>

My friend sent me that word. She<br/>
and I have only truly begun to touch each other<br/>

within the last month, but<br/>
already she knows loving an other<br/>

means loving what their existence evokes<br/>
from your own--the songs you hope they haven't<br/>

heard before, the verbs that roll velvet <br/>
off your tongue and only now have an address<br/>

to be addressed to; the way your headspace is <br/>
no longer all your own but a little less lonely for it.<br/>

Let me love my cold brain's leaking.<br/>
Let me love you the way I love my self,<br/>

less an active act of love than a <br/>
simple statement of being,<br/>

and the world the way I love you. I know<br/>
there is no space for beauty in this new year<br/>

without the erosion of the old, I know<br/>
you will be but warm vapor seeping slowly<br/>

from my ear come next January, but for now, please,<br/>
let me love the building up of all these beautiful<br/>

words <br/>
without mourning the silence their ablation will leave<br/>
