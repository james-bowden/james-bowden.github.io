## September afternoon, at four o'clock
#### Marge Piercy 

Full in the hand, heavy<br/>
with ripeness, perfume spreading<br/>
its fan: moments now resemble<br/>
sweet russet pears glowing<br/>
on the bough, peaches warm<br/>
from the afternoon sun, amber<br/>
and juicy, flesh that can<br/>
make you drunk.<br/><br/>

There is a turn in things<br/>
that makes the heart catch.<br/>
We are ripening, all the hard<br/>
green grasping, the stony will<br/>
swelling into sweetness, the acid<br/>
and sugar in balance, the sun<br/>
stored as energy that is pleasure<br/>
and pleasure that is energy.<br/><br/>

Whatever happens, whatever,<br/>
we say, and hold hard and let<br/>
go and go on. In the perfect<br/>
moment the future coils,<br/>
a tree inside a pit. Take,<br/>
eat, we are each other's<br/>
perfection, the wine of our<br/>
mouths is sweet and heavy.<br/>
Soon enough comes the vinegar.<br/>
The fruit is ripe for the taking<br/>
and we take. There is<br/>
no other wisdom.<br/><br/>
