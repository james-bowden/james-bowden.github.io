## The summer of my loneliness,
#### James Bowden

and it's not even really summer yet, still spring, but<br/>
the socal sun has a way of eating into you, distorting<br/>
time whichever way you'd least prefer. At times I want<br/>
to write to you about how there's nothing going on,<br/>
how available I am and nobody to let me love them,<br/>
just books and air, heavy with heat, to hold me. What<br/>
is it about complaining that's so cathartic? But this<br/>
suggests release and the truth is much more clingy,<br/>
conversing like consolidation of emotional intensity. And<br/>
will you blame me for using you? And will you harbor<br/>
my weakness against me? No matter--I don't need<br/>
you *that* much. Not enough to compromise myself.<br/>
And so it's no wonder I'm so alone, I'm smelling every<br/>
rose on campus but especially the red ones tinged<br/>
with enough purple to make them look luscious, <br/>
they're fragrant without fail, and, me. What does<br/>
my face say? Would you get close enough to inhale<br/>
me? I even use fragrance-free everything, so that <br/>
you might know me, and this is just spitballing now<br/>
and everyone knows nothing comes from nothingness,<br/>
I'm coping, I'm standing in a field surrounded by <br/>
acres of open air except I'm in the middle of Pasadena<br/>
on a college campus all the same and I'm the brightest<br/>
thing I can see for miles. I like you too. What I'm<br/>
trying to ask every time I talk to somebody is: can<br/>
you let me be free, and love me too? Help me, even?<br/>
I know I'm trying to do so for you, and this, among<br/>
other reasons, is likely why you have left me, why I<br/>
let you leave so lightly, I don't believe I've any right<br/>
to hold anyone even if it be their will and I don't know,<br/>
I could trace this back to my father but that's beside <br/>
the point. I just miss you and don't want to sound so <br/>
clumsily desperate. I want you to understand how<br/>
I need you without trying to make you stay. *What<br/>
good is freedom*, you ask--so that we may choose<br/>
to be bound, and have it mean something. So that<br/>
our lightness may have weight and our weight not<br/>
be so light, so chance. Which is to say, I am lonely<br/>
by choice,&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;but also out of need.<br/><br/>
