## Watching you talk on the phone, I consider the empty space around atoms--
#### Rhiannon McGavin

how the particles that seed all matter are mostly void. Each nucleus is a maypole<br/>
for its electrons to circle, & their negative charges repel other electrons that spin<br/>
in other fields so the ribbon paths never kiss, only overlap, which means nothing<br/>
really touches– rain & dirt, apron strings, the phone nestled between your neck<br/>

& shoulder as you look for the pasta strainer. You wave one hand like a child<br/>
playing conductor, & this flail proves you’re not lying when your mother asks<br/>
about your day from upstate. When I was a crush, I’d watch you step away<br/>
downstairs to run this vaudeville routine, but you take the calls next to me now.<br/>

Your family pops through the window, stirs a pot, adds more salt. I am enough<br/>
of you to warrant this flavor of intimacy, these homeward sounds, for my own<br/>
mother to fret about how skinny you are. To make my birthday cake from scratch,<br/>
you wouldn’t just plant strawberries: you’d create another universe. I wanted you<br/>

warm and close as fresh laundry and here we are, Tuesday.<br/>
Of course you love me, you’re wearing one of my socks.<br/>

---
The last line here is one of my favorite lines in all the poetry I've ever read. I wear this sock quite often.<br/>
