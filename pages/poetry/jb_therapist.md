## My therapist tries to touch me
#### James Bowden

*for Denise Lin*<br/><br/>

*I never asked for flight. Only to feel this fully, this entire,<br/>
the way snow touched bare skin & is, suddenly, snow no longer.*  <br/>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—Ocean Vuong, “Devotion”<br/><br/>

Come. Close your eyes. Walk. I am here<br/>
with You, along your forearm. I am here<br/>
with You, a voice just ahead. How<br/>
(*how*) does it feel? How<br/>
(*how*) do You feel?<br/><br/>

Not the blindness itself, but the blinding,<br/>
Nor the going, but the letting, Let go, Let go,<br/>
Let go, Let go, Open <br/>
those eyes, Your eyes. <br/>
You, alive. You. You!<br/><br/>

(*Rilke rolls in his rotted coffin*)<br/>
(*No feeling is final*)<br/>
Walk. Pause. Let go. She smiles at me<br/>
as if that <br/>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;is enough. <br/>
As if I ( *i* )<br/>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;am enough. <br/><br/>

<hr>

Draws from Rilke's classic ["Go to the Limits of Your Longing"]([https://www.amywoschekschmidt.com/poetry/abundance](https://onbeing.org/poetry/go-to-the-limits-of-your-longing/)). I had fun with this one, based on a good real experience. I also gave it to my therapist, and this was one of the main ways she came to realize how much she meant, means to me. Silliness but also love, so.
