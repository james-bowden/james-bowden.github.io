## Telemachus' Telemachus
#### James Bowden

I have written 800 posts on my private Instagram now, private to me and some ten<br/>
others I have decided to share shreds of my internal life with. Eight hundreds. Bleh. I<br/>

miss you, where you = many people, you included too, but especially those that I've<br/>
loved and lost lately. "Lost" alone being too strong a word, so instead, qualify it with<br/>

*lost, at least in the capacity I'm used to having you in*. Which is to say, currently, you<br/>
= {Kristine, Antonia, Rachel, etc.}. And it isn't like *that*, so don't you think that, I just<br/>

miss how life was with various people during various periods. Don't you? But here's <br/>
the crux of the matter: when I've the choice, I always choose to rip myself open and<br/>

hemorrhage hurt. So let's begin again: I was listening to Conan Gray's "Astronomy" in <br/>
the Philadelphia train station today. Rachel gifted me that song in severance some<br/>

two springs ago. I paid too much for a stupid Amtrak ticket: inelastic demand. Transp<br/>
ortation always reminds me of missing and leaving people but especially Rachel. Of <br/>

being left, as people depart for things more important than me. I am still shouting in<br/>
to the dark: *What is of more consequence than a lover? What matters more than a<br/>*

*son?* Thankfully, nobody hears me. Not even my ten constituents--they don't really<br/>
read most of my posts anyway. Nobody really wants to get that tangled up in some<br/>

one else's consciousness, though it's easy to think that you do. The question, then, is: <br/>
do you feel abandon or relief? My answer leaps out through my eyes, before I've even <br/>

managed to seal my lips. Transportation often feels like crying to me. Tears like trains <br/>
like texts like telemachus like falling asleep alone with someone in mind, perhaps with <br/>

the Archduke Trio playing. Like Tchaikovsky's violin concerto playing now, Joshua Bell. <br/>
And this is not about Rachel, but rather the inevitability of self. Apathy in antithesis.<br/>

Reticence in retrograde. And what do we do with that? *How* do we do with that? I <br/>
think about filling holes, how I've been shittily filling the ones in my heart whenever <br/>

I can but failing to find any lasting balm, each patch poking holes anew. Adhesion: a <br/>
belief in permanence. I know that the self persists. I want to be gorgeous. I want to<br/>

be beautiful, burn bright and lively. I want to love everything and everyone I can<br/>
manage. And here, today, I am what I want. How do we deal though, how do we<br/>

bear it? I used to pray that I live in terms of presences rather than absences, but it <br/>
turns out that to hold is to let go. To need, to not have. No thing without nothing, <br/>

no self without you. My train arrives back in Princeton and suddenly I am the<br/>
conductor. Faces pop in and out. You you you you. My arms grow weary of the<br/>

reins. The baton turns to lead between my fingers. The train to coal and dream<br/>
scape. When I wake up, I am on a dinky little island and I pull at my face in the<br/>

mirror. Gentle waves lap at the mountain of Dilaudid beside me. I am once again,<br/>
unbearably alone. I sew my eyes shut that I may begin to face myself now.<br/><br/>

<hr>

Draws from Ocean Vuong's and Louise Gluck's Telemachus poems, and *Infinite Jest*. And a lil *Uncle Vanya* from Chekhov, mainly the *bear it* bit.
