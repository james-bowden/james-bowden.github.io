## Of course
#### James Bowden

*for Tanner*

What a blessing it is that we’ve been taught <br/>
Not to reveal the true extent <br/>
Of those dangerous feelings <br/>
For then we’d face many more <br/>
Much more concrete <br/>
failures, rejections <br/>
No, we must be careful <br/>
Better to not know than to know what we want not <br/>
And thus even as I gaze at you <br/>
Swat the ball back and forth like a hot potato <br/>
We cannot bring ourselves to admit <br/>
That which we both know, both hope <br/>
And when I ask you to do <br/>
I expect nothing more than an <br/>
*I guess <br/>
Sure* <br/>
But in your eyes I see <br/>
That you have been waiting <br/>
For me <br/>
And I no longer need to hear it from your mouth <br/>
Those spheres tell a story fuller than you or I can claim to know <br/>
Pray we let it run its course <br/>
And suddenly we’re out of time <br/>
Those nights at the table over <br/>
As our sleepy eyes kiss goodbye once more<br/> 
I cannot bring my body to convey <br/>
The part of me that breaks and leaves as you do <br/>
But it does nonetheless <br/>
Whether I tell you or not <br/>
And I know you’ll take good care of it <br/>
I hope. <br/>

<hr>

I'm entertained, looking at this several years later, how much this reads like a romantic love poem of sorts when really it's for an old best friend (a guy). I don't mind it--in my mind, loving a friend is not far off from romantic love, at least in magnitude of connection.

It also reminds me of how many boys/men just don't learn to have/acknowledge/communicate feelings (often learn only to repress them), even though I think this poem describes something beautiful that came about in part through that sad cultural phenomenon. I hope it continues to roll back, and I shall try to help however I can.
