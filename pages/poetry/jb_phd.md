## Pray there shall be poetry in my computer science PhD
#### James Bowden 

I am so excited for fall to fall upon me.<br/><br/>

Summer has been sweet, but havoc wreaks<br/>
my subconscious. It is said that to do great<br/>
work, the brain must be loosed upon it like<br/>
a wild dog. The pieces I can drag together<br/>
consciously, dwarfed by what my neurons<br/>
synthesize behind my back and proffer me,<br/>
small-handed and mysterious, in the shower,<br/>
in dreams, in the hills that stretch like wheat-<br/>
elephants from the high glass window of<br/>
my desk. With the flurry of change that is <br/>
fall: imagine how many fluttering leaves my<br/>
brain will have to chew on. Imagine how my<br/>
turmoil will be matched by the biting wind. Imagine<br/>
all the alliteration all the assonance all the<br/>
all the albumin that my ærs may or may<br/>
not pick up on. Where, oh where, will go<br/>
the syllabic little slut that is, me?<br/><br/>

Am I a poet? Am I a mathematician? Am I simply<br/>
concussed? Is my brain big enough to straddle the<br/>
line, all the lines? Balance does **not**<br/>
become me. I push my subconscious toward the scientific,<br/>
and the poetry of life threatens to slip through my fingers.<br/>
Emotions I have fought so hard to feel seem quickly<br/>
irrelevant. Only <br/>
focus. Only machine. The<br/>
the beautiful borne upon me bears past me and I <br/>
I blink.<br/>
I can only hold so much. What if I can't hold it all?<br/><br/>

I cannot hold it all.<br/>
