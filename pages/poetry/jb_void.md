## voioioioioid
#### James Bowden

*for **you** and **you** and **my father** and **you** and **you***<br/>


**if everything is bolded, then nothing is bolded**<br/>
if my father is correct, **everything** matters--every last drop of honey<br/>
if **nothing** is bolded, how shall we navigate the **dark**?<br/>
our eyes adjust to the **void**.<br/>

if my father correct: **every** last taste matters<br/>
I want to try and be terrific, like I was before: even if only for **an hour**.<br/>
my eyes adjust to the **dark**.<br/>
I glimpse my reflection in the **black** of phone--no longer your wonting irises<br/>

**every** hour spent with you, I **swear** I was terrific<br/>
they say that the need for new **love** is faithfulness to the old<br/>
I cannot see **my reflection** in your eyes any longer; as such the **blackness** beckons<br/>
, becomes **me**, I *tip tap* into the **void**, chiseling portrait out of **absence** with but my thumbs<br/>

**The need for new love is faithfulness to the old.** This I know; this I know--<br/>
each relationship, a **proof of concept**<br/>
I am becoming **me, emptiness** in effigy<br/>
and your tears kiss my shoulder, then *plink, plink* into my heel.<br/>

--<br/>
*for ~~a **happy** ending~~ PG-13 audiences, turn to page 3.<br/>
for **truth**, continue.*<br/>
--<br/>

You are but a proof of concept<br/> 
that **I am a real boy--**<br/>
your pain, gasoline into my tank<br/>
my tongue, my phosphorous head<br/>

**I am a real boy<br/>
You are all my toys<br/>
My cousins put firecrackers in their plastic dinosaurs for the new year<br/>
and I watched, wooden, unwillingly from the washroom window upon the toilet cover**<br/>

**something**, anything **to** burn **the** dark<br/>
my **eyes** swivel **back** into **the** dark<br/>
**a** *fwoosh*, **an** extended **arm** against **the** dark<br/>
if **you** are **light**, then **I** must **be** dark<br/>

--<br/>
page 3<br/>

our eyes adjust to the dark<br/>
our eyes adjust to the bright<br/>
if nothing is you, then everything me<br/>
if everything honey, you are nothing to me<br/>

<hr>

So when I wrote this, Jenny, my poetry professor, pointed out the sort of literary baggage that light and dark, black and white carry and I do agree--I don't want to contribute to that, and see where this poem can be viewed that way. Obviously, I wrote it without that intention at all, and am not sure how to particularly redo this without the use of that theme, so I'm leaving it here for the nice parts and such.

This poem was written with a rather anguished tone in my head that I am not capable of recreating (or just refuse to try) in voice, but unsure how much that comes through.
