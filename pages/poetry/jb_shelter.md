## I could never lie about caring a lot
#### James Bowden

To myself.<br/>
What have I become? What am I becoming?<br/>
I don’t want to care. It’s too much work. <br/>
But without it I am nothing. <br/>

I want to live in the moment. To value the process and forgo the end. To not care about the end, because no<br/>
matter how hard I try, I do. But how can I value the process if I suspect it’s leading me straight over a cliff?<br/>

I have all of these ideals. All these feelings, desires, thoughts. And yet, what it always seems to come down to<br/>
is intimacy. Or the lack thereof. <br/>
Intimacy is an end as well. But also a process. I hate commitment. <br/>

I can’t deal with this. Or I don’t want to. It’s hard.<br/>
I know the good things in life come from hard work but dammit, I’m tired. So what if all I want is a pair of<br/>
loving arms to wrap around me and reassure me that I have value without greatness, that I deserve to be loved<br/>
regardless of how hard I work and what I accomplish. I certainly don’t believe that for myself, though I could<br/>
for another. <br/>

We can’t all be great. <br/>
We can all love and be loved. <br/>
We all love greatness.<br/>

I just want to say Fuck it, and do. And be. And love. And be loved. Is it wrong to <br/>
love being loved? Is it wrong to be so damn human that I care and don’t care and feel and hate and desire and yearn and despise and struggle<br/>

and give up?<br/>

What if that’s all we can do?<br/>

<hr>

The title comes from the end of a lovely Dermot Kennedy song, [Shelter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efhSV8HMRDQ). I particularly like the end of the poem as a way to move forward, though I'm not sure it was that when I first wrote it, more of a defeat probably. But then again, maybe that's the point anyway.
