## Cold
#### Ellen Bass

On this early morning in Vancouver, my son and I stop<br/>
on our way to breakfast when we hear<br/>
the Kenyan will soon be running past this corner.<br/>
Of course we want to see his gorgeous stride,<br/>
but after half an hour I'm shivering<br/>
in my thin sweater. That's when my son begins<br/>
to rub my back--offering up the heat of his palms.<br/>
What could be better than to stand here hungry<br/>
and be curried like this? If I hadn't been cold<br/>
I wouldn't have his hands on my spine,<br/>
flaring across my shoulder blades. For a moment<br/>
it seems possible that every frailty, every pain,<br/>
could be an opening, a crack that lets the unexpected<br/>
reach us. How can I remember this<br/>
when I'm old and need so much?<br/>
