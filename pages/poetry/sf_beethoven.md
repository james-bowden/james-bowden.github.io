## Beethoven
#### Shangyang Fang

Deep autumn, my neighbor starts to wonder<br/>
how am I going to live. I want to die.<br/>
Life has depleted its sustenance. No telephone.<br/>
No texts from you. I listen to Beethoven,<br/>
learning how to go on even if I'm betrayed<br/>
by the present. In two hours<br/>
it's going to rain. The streets will be completely bare.<br/>
The world grotesque like a stretcher. <br/>
Future full of meanness, vatic with voids.<br/>
Late sonatas of Beethoven are different<br/>
from his earlier works, his more familiar,<br/>
famous lessons on strength and light.<br/>
Pedagogical arpeggios. Polished by time<br/>
and torments of time, like a leaf stuck<br/>
in the windowpane, Sonata no. 31, for example,<br/>
is but a magpie with its wings and tail chopped off.<br/>
Even if you don't want life, you live by eating,<br/>
sleeping, loving without an object.<br/>
You pray and your prayer comes true. All this<br/>
is but a dream of feathers. I wait for the pianist's<br/>
fingers to slide back into her pockets.<br/>
