## Individuality Study
#### James Bowden

1. This picture makes me want to die of cuteness every time I see it. There’s a train. There’s my child <br/>
brother. His oversized shirt commands \*stand by me\* as the train speeds toward the onlooker, perhaps it <br/>
begs, though the thick bubble font suggests otherwise. The train, coming right through his belly. <br/>
2. Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I shall fear no evil[, for You are with me];<br/>
3. David Foster Wallace has this line, or I should say David Lipsky gives him this line in his live-in interview <br/>
piece, about his worldview falling apart. Postmodernist crisis. How he felt better than everyone for seeing<br/>
through the delusion, but much worse because he couldn’t function under those premises. I used to laugh <br/>
at the caption to the far right side of the IQ bell curve graphic: “yours to lose”. I admit that I found Ethan <br/>
Hawke more likable than Jude Law in *Gattaca*. Suddenly, I am the uselessest person I know. <br/>
4. And so it turns out there is no such thing as evil: just nothingness. All bad, just misdirected intensity. <br/>
Intensity: somethingness. No such thing as valley or shadow or death: just lack. No lines. All, this. <br/>
5. So we have only lack. Well, that’s alright, darling: Ikea sells lack for $14.99, and that ought to tide us <br/>
over for a meal or two, though no more given inflation. All I’ve ever wanted is a roof and some food <br/>
anyway, with some consistency guarantee. Guarantee—that’s where this insecurity began! As if anything <br/>
could be enough to fix anything with absolute certainty in this [economy]<br/>
6. And so we are left to beg for a train. A crane? Anything to pierce this silence. Have you read *Waiting <br/>
for Godot*? I know I am loath to be given a train. Instead, I ask for you. <br/>
7. Can anyone be both the light & what reflects it? I ask. I ask, I ask, I ask. <br/>
8. I do admit singularity. I do admit need. The question, then, is: where does the light begin? from whence <br/>
does it come? me, or you, or John Steinbeck, or every suicidal writer, or Mariah Carey? Probably <br/>
commercialism. Naïveté in any form possible. Have you seen people on Black Friday? The way their eyes[ light up]--
