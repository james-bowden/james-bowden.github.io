## Mama
#### James Bowden

I don’t know what she was wearing the last time I saw her. Not that it matters. <br/>
I don’t know if I shall ever see her again. Although I expect it. <br/>
What if she doesn’t make it back <br/>
Or worse <br/>
What if she does but is too far gone <br/>
It’s in our genes. <br/>

Is it wrong that I only feel bad because I know <br/>
That she knows what I was wearing the last time she saw me. That it does matter. To her. <br/>
That she doesn’t know if she’ll ever see me again. Although she hopes for it. <br/>
And what if she doesn’t make it back? <br/>
Or is too far gone <br/>
For I know her biggest regret <br/>
Would be not having spoken to me that one last time. <br/>

Can I not love immaterially, unconditionally? Maybe that’s something we can only do after the fact. Once the <br/>
person has been reduced to an idea. <br/>
A person is human. I don’t get along with humans. <br/>
An idea is whatever you make of it. I know what I’ll choose to make of hers. <br/>
But why wait until it’s too late? <br/>
I think <br/>
I’ll call her now. <br/>

I don't think I've ever called my mom "mama", but it seemed to convey the right sentiment.
