## That's one small step for man
#### James Bowden

They really said, *we're putting a poem on the moooon*,<br/>
I read this in M's voice, thank you for this gift of inflection,<br/>
I've been thinking too much about how each person<br/>
touches my life of late, trying to dissect the meat of<br/>
each interaction, turn stardust to dirt, and OK. So it<br/>
turns out all of it really is just moondirt, but the kids<br/>
don't have to know that, I don't have to know that, <br/>
don't want to, I'm trying to live a poetic life over here,<br/>
still hold the dirt, I mean stardust, but let it slip<br/>
through my fingers and tell myself I meant to let it go.<br/>
It's a bloody mess up there. An asteroid belt of phrases<br/>
and sounds and knowing that Ada Limón's poem will<br/>
be on the Europa Clipper, which my ex designed landing<br/>
gear for back when we were still in love. A wobbling<br/>
singularity eats itself at the middle. I wonder if I can<br/>
model my thoughts with Hamiltonian dynamics, arcing<br/>
about the surface of spacetime and crashing into Pluto.<br/>
I've a few planets, fortunately, but they won't talk to me<br/>
anymore and exist but behind my eyelids. No matter--<br/>
I loose my neurons like a particle swarm each morning, each<br/>
empty moment really, and your orbit brings us into<br/>
contact about once every week. And I wish I could<br/>
deliver a poem to you but I think we'd both burn up<br/>
upon reentry and besides, your planetary defense <br/>
systems are much stronger than my grin these days.<br/>
But if I could, if, I'd lie flat against your dusty surface.<br/>
Arms wide. I'd promise to try to embrace all of you. Well,<br/>
and then I'd bounce around joyfully in the levity of our <br/>
gravity.<br/><br/>
