## Wistful sounds like a brand of air freshener
#### Bob Hicok

I will go to Belfast, Maine, and read my poetry to crabs.<br/>
I’ll stand on a platform of some kind in the company of wind<br/>
and look at pennants waving and think of the claws<br/>
of crabs waving in the wind of the Atlantic and be sad.<br/>
It’s not that I don’t have enough sadness, but I’m always looking<br/>
for better, more aquatic or tastier sadness, for the kind<br/>
of light that comes when the sky tilts its head at dusk<br/>
and wonders, in colors we understand as language, why this all<br/>
has to end. I could doff a Bogart hat and wag a tough cigarette<br/>
between my lips, smoke muscling up from my mouth as I say, it just does,<br/>
sweetheart, it just does, but the psychology of the fedora<br/>
escapes me. There’s bread and calisthenics and lice and radar<br/>
and jars of blue stuff in stores, and maybe what I’m doing<br/>
when I cry to certain songs at seventy miles an hour, is proving<br/>
I’ve noticed that out of the nothing that could be here,<br/>
everything is. So I will go to Belfast, Maine, and wonder<br/>
what it’s like to stand beside Main Street in the winter,<br/>
I’ll put my head against the brick buildings I’m betting<br/>
live there year-round and describe the tropics to them<br/>
by having warm thoughts, and if you’d like to meet me there,<br/>
I’ll be the man in the t-shirt that has an extra sleeve<br/>
in case the third arm I need shows up, because so far,<br/>
I’ve dropped almost everything I’m desperate to hold.<br/>
