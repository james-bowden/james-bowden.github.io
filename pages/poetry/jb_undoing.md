## *Choose your undoing*, Ellie Sawatzky urges.
#### James Bowden

*Perhaps a goat chews your corset strings loose.* Oh no! I've a normal-sized tummy!<br/>
I, the goat, rather than the corset-wearer: you, the delicate balance between <br/>
love of another and love of self and love of self because an other whom<br/>
I love loves that self and all this love just circular and <br/>
supported by no haunches other than my own--<br/>
I used to dream that you would talk to me.<br/>
I used to conflate Dermot Kennedy lines<br/>
with what we'd had and could have had.<br/>
Step back, love: this is my popcorn chicken.<br/>
This is my heart beating out of my chest, my rainy<br/>
morning and my favorite poems. After this long winter of <br/>
your silence, I am falling for a new version of my self, one who<br/>
struggles to catch his breath whenever the cute blonde girl he's falling for<br/>
utters that smooth, low, musical note in her register that probably no one else<br/>
notices. Whenever she smiles sunshine and reminds me of the child-like playfulness<br/>
I left with you in the aftermath. I listened to Joji's *Glimpse of Us*, several times, like you'd<br/>
have wanted me to, and for the first time, didn't feel that familiar tightening of the chest. The<br/>
only tightening, hope. The only solidity, me and mine. I don't believe that we ever choose our undoing, <br/>
more an inability to unchoose. As if I could have not chosen you. As if I would not choose her. Come, darling.<br/>

<hr>

A good amount of sacrilege here. Notice the switch in the *you* occurring at the very last line. Not sure if this reflects real life, but thought it a nice concept.
