## ***DO NOT LEAVE VALUABLES IN VEHICLE***
#### James Bowden

Today, I'm on top of a hill. Tomorrow, at the bottom of the sea, but let's leave that aside for now, shan't we?<br/>
Today I am on top of a hill. <br/>
There's a Holocaust memorial, bodies of plaster twisted on the floor and on each other<br/>
while a lone Jew looks out through some not-so-endless barbed wire that is not-so-tied around some <br/>
not-so-real tree trunks. Rather skinny ones at that, as if they must match their captives' emaciation.<br/>
That's my mom's grandma on the floor there. <br/>
Don't worry, *my* grandma survived the Holocaust, even if just long enough to make me possible and get hit by <br/>
a car 14 months later. <br/>
So it goes.<br/>

Grand theft auto. A fine name for a video game, and for the hasty getaway those men made, one wearing<br/>
some sort of ski mask, and I'm not even sure the other was indeed a man.<br/>
I saw them break the car window and drive off with it, the confirmed man halfway out the window and waving <br/>
in victory and in *fuck you*. Come to think of it, I didn't see them break the car window, but I heard the victim<br/>
say it.<br/>
Picture this: a rather effeminate male voice, repeatedly exclaiming, *Babe, oh my god*<br/>
and *what did they get???* and *they took your phone??* and *they took your purse too?! what was in it?* and <br/>
another *Babe, oh my god* and perhaps a *We need to call the cops, babe, babe! <br/>
Babe, oh my god!* The aforenamed Babe, a girl in a puffy white dress that isn't quite a wedding dress but could<br/>
be. I think the car was hers, not his, but he is still the vessel for emotion, and *Babe, oh my god!* The police <br/>
come rushing up, one car, then another, and I can't imagine what they'll say or do. In my perverse mind, they <br/>
point to the sign and shrug, maybe laugh if their parents were assholes too. <br/>
They'll have to dump the car somewhere anyway, right? Let us look for it then, I suppose.<br/>
They just wanted to take some pictures.<br/>
Maybe they cannot pay for the wedding anymore after replacing the phone and the laptop and the bag and <br/>
the dress not to mention the car but then again maybe they were not getting married anyway for what Babe <br/>
wore didn't look like much of a wedding dress to me<br/>
not that I am a credible source.<br/>
So it goes?<br/>

Ain't it lovely that we needn't turn to fiction for absurdity? That I could put Vonnegut out of a job, were he still <br/>
alive? And really, it does go so. <br/><br/>


It's foggy as hell and I'm bored. I don't care how smart these people are: I'm pining and horny. It's nice outside <br/>
and there's plenty to do and nobody to do it with.<br/>

Isn't it telling that I'm more envious of Richard Brautigan and his poet-in-residence spot than anyone else I've <br/>
heard of at Caltech? Today, maybe, I am on a hill, but certainly not a mountain, and I confessed that writing <br/>
some nice stuff would make me indelibly happy<br/>
Now, that would put me on a mountain. <br/>
It scares me how certain I am of that. And how unprepared and unable I feel, how trapped I feel for someone <br/>
with alleged infinite potential. <br/>
Maybe I can just do it on the side. Yet this is both proof that I can, and a suggestion that I probably won't. <br/>

Isn't it a scary thought that I might be my own muse? That I haven't even a spiky (or should I say *shrike*-y), <br/>
imposing muse like Martin Silenus, or even half as many years to write for that matter?<br/>

I'd very much like to conclude with a fat <br/>

***DON'T HAVE VALUABLES***<br/>

for then, surely, we would not leave them in our vehicles, nor lose them.<br/>

But as someone with neither a vehicle nor valuables, this strikes me as a very unsatisfying conclusion. Instead, I <br/>
will end with<br/>

***HAVE AS MANY VALUABLES AS HUMANLY POSSIBLE***<br/>

for then you may lose as many as is possible, and that shellshocked man and the fact that he can say *Babe, oh<br/>
my god, Babe*, now *that*, not Brautigan's position itself,<br/>
that is truly enviable.<br/>
