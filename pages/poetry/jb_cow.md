## Cow days
#### James Bowden 

Sometimes<br/>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I feel like a cow<br/>
What with how often I am called to milk myself.<br/>
I have built up enough material for a poem,<br/>
and so forth it must come. What I mean is:<br/>
I feel a great sadness. I should just keep grazing,<br/>
anything to keep my head tamped down, but my teat <br/>
feels like it’s going to explode and I can’t sit still, I rove <br/>
and run like a bitch in a heat. I plod down the street<br/>
and there’s a board proclaiming, *I love because* <br/>
*I* am *love*, and I think, *wow, hey, that’s just like*<br/>
*me, I am love*, and I redouble my resolve to<br/>
smile at each person I come into contact with<br/>
but a Chinese woman walks by me with sunnies<br/>
and her head doesn’t even tilt a millimeter. It’s hard,<br/>
I say, to love what won’t even look you in the eye. I<br/>
am not so sure that I am even conscious in many of the<br/>
in-between moments, and maybe this is good and then<br/>
maybe this is excuse enough for us all. *It’s too hard*<br/>
*to be alive all the time*, autopilot saves us, TV saves us,<br/>
do we live for each moment?, or the in-betweens,<br/>
or for those cool, dry seconds of clarity when I <br/>
inhale and know that I, *I!*, am here, cockpit and <br/>
helm and all? I’m playing with my beard and thinking<br/>
of my little brother, how delighted he would be to<br/>
run his damp little fingers through the course ropes of<br/>
my chin, tickling me where I am weak, *my neck*.<br/>
Charlie Brown says that happiness is a warm puppy<br/>
and I say it’s you, hot cheek brushing against me<br/>
in your dreamy bliss, eyelids heavy with trust. *Just* be<br/>
*here. Just be* here. Remember *The Notebook*? Once, <br/>
in the simmer after much strife, I told my Indian ex-<br/>
girlfriend that if she was a beef, I was a beef. We <br/>
stitched ourselves silly and by god, we loved. I hope <br/>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;when I die <br/>
they make me into chicken tikka masala. Am I allowed<br/>
to bequeath that? But excuse me: I must go to milk <br/>
myself. Excuse me: I’m thinking of starting to talk<br/>
aloud to myself, so that I may understand that I<br/>
am separate from myself. So that when they take<br/>
me away, I won’t ruin the dish by kicking and getting<br/>
lactic acid everywhere. Do you under stand me?<br/>
I am looking for a basis for acceptance of every,<br/>
like really every, thing. Excuse me: did you know<br/>
that Charlie Parker played Bossa Nova? Did you<br/>
know that my favorite musician is ashamed of<br/>
his too-idyllic, white, Wisconsinian childhood?<br/>
Should he be? The low sounds he makes vibrate<br/>
in the hollow of my chest and: comfort. Has anyone<br/> 
the right to be ashamed of anything? The butcher?<br/>
The hand, rough, pumping me dry? Excuse me!<br/>
What I mean to say is: you’re on your way to<br/>
visit the plum trees. And you want to be a skeptic<br/>
and you want to be like *hey is this even real* and <br/>
*hey, what’s the big deal with all of this anyway* but<br/>
then you get there and blossoms are swirling <br/>
around into everywhere and bees buzz scary but<br/>
don’t sting your toughened hide and when you go to<br/>
open your mouth, all that comes out is a rumbling <br/>
in your chest, a purring, a sigh, a moo.<br/>
