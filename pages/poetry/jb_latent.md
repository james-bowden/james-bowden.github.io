## I analyze/lament my native distribution
#### James Bowden

I haven't been able to write much lately, but maybe<br/>
I just needed concreteness. So begin again: your lips<br/>
braced against the cold. There are flowers on the floor!<br/>
The office ceiling isn't closed and smacks of Costco.<br/>
There are pipes marked storm drain all over the place. I<br/>
suppose you would want a lot of them, if ever it were to<br/>
storm. I'm trying to read papers, but my eyelids are heavy<br/>
and I think I did get enough sleep. I could fill in my finances<br/>
spreadsheet, there's a couple in front of me tuning their<br/>
voices together in passing and hers is glassy in a way that<br/>
makes me shiver now, trying to describe it for you. Now<br/>
they've parted and gone. I'm wide awake writing, though<br/>
I know I should be doing *something* productive. I keep on<br/>
using the machine learning I learn to frame my own mess<br/>
of a life. For example, lately reading about balancing<br/>
the natural, global, realistic distribution over data that a<br/>
model learns with optimizing an objective. How to<br/>
reconcile optimal with plausible, reasonable? When left to<br/>
my own devices I largely write, marinate in thoughts, sip<br/>
sadness, write some more, and read books. Walk around.<br/>
Inhale flowers, as it were. Now it looks like I'm involved,<br/>
though, and must be productive. How can I combine this<br/>
with that? Should I? Is everything a question of constructing<br/>
something close to natural that can also be useful?<br/>
For those of us who are lucky enough to even think<br/>
of being natural, I mean. For whom natural and useful<br/>
have not been made forcefully inseparable.<br/>
